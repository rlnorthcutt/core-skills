# Productivity, in practice

Productivity advice is everywhere, and most of it is the same three points: prioritize, cut distractions, take breaks. That's fine as far as it goes, but it's the mindset underneath that actually moves the needle.

The useful version of "growth mindset" here is concrete: pick the one task that matters most today and do it before you check anything. Focus is a habit you build by removing options, not a switch you flip. When you stop treating your attention as something to be split, the work gets noticeably smoother.

Consistency beats intensity. Small habits compound, and the systems that survive are the boring ones — a short list, a hard stop, a default "no."

I don't have a study to cite for this, and I'm not going to pretend I do. What I've seen is that people who write down three priorities each morning and actually respect them get more done than people who buy a productivity app.

So: fewer frameworks, more doing. Start with the one thing you'd be embarrassed to have not done by Friday, and do that first.
